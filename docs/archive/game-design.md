# I Am Not a Robot

## Game Design Document — Working Draft

> Historical draft. The [current session direction](./session-direction.md)
> supersedes conflicting measurement, rhythm, session, and presentation proposals
> below. Retained as a source of earlier ideas.

## 1. High concept

**I Am Not a Robot** is a 20–25 minute erotic hypnosis game disguised as a human-verification system.

The player begins by completing CAPTCHA-style tests intended to prove that they are human. Through repetition, the tests make their responses faster, more predictable, and more automatic. The system uses that behavior to establish that the player has become a robot.

Becoming a robot is the transition into the game’s main act. Once the player is a robot, the verification system becomes a programming interface. It uses visual patterns, finger-tracing exercises, repetition, reward signals, and self-descriptive CAPTCHA statements to program the player’s attention, movement, expectations, and obedience.

The dramatic progression is:

> **I am not a robot.**
> **I am becoming a robot.**
> **I am a robot.**
> **I am being programmed.**
> **I am being programmed to obey.**
> **I am programmed.**

The primary fantasy is not merely arriving at a programmed state. It is **experiencing the programming as it happens**.

---

## 2. Player experience

The player should feel that:

1. They began by consciously solving simple tests.
2. Repetition taught them the interface’s patterns.
3. Their hand began moving before they had fully evaluated each prompt.
4. The system became increasingly able to predict their behavior.
5. Selecting “I am a robot” felt like an accurate description of what had happened.
6. Becoming a robot allowed the system to begin explicitly programming them.
7. Each programming cycle made a response feel easier, more expected, and less separate from the instruction that produced it.
8. By the end, instructions, desires, responses, and affirmations have partially collapsed into one another.

The uploaded reference script succeeds by directing attention toward tiny ordinary experiences—considering an answer, clicking it, noticing which feelings arise—and then reinterpreting those experiences as the beginning of conditioning. 

The game should do the same thing mechanically. It should not merely say that programming is occurring; it should repeatedly create something the player can recognize as a learned response.

---

## 3. Design pillars

### 3.1 The mechanic must enact the fantasy

Every major late-game statement should correspond to something the player has just done.

> “My finger follows the pattern” follows a tracing task.
> “The cue produces the response” follows a cue-only test.
> “I respond before I finish thinking” follows a rapid habitual selection.
> “I am being programmed” follows a complete learning cycle.
> “I am being programmed to obey” follows the player executing a programmed instruction.

### 3.2 Robot transformation precedes programming

The structure must remain explicit:

* The player first proves that they are human.
* The player then becomes a robot.
* Only after becoming a robot does programming begin.
* Programming is the substantial third act, not an epilogue or final reveal.

### 3.3 Use one narrow interaction language

The game should not become a collection of unrelated CAPTCHA minigames.

Its two core interaction families are:

* **CAPTCHA selection:** read, classify, select.
* **Path tracing:** touch, follow, hold, release.

These actions recur throughout the whole experience and acquire new meanings over time.

### 3.4 Preserve uncertainty about authorship

The player should know that they are being programmed, but remain uncertain about the boundary between:

* choosing and following,
* trying and responding automatically,
* agreeing with a statement and giving the expected answer,
* wanting the reward and wanting to obey,
* consciously helping the process and having the process happen to them.

The reference script explicitly builds its fantasy around comparing intentional action with something that seems to happen on its own. 

### 3.5 Reward migrates from correctness to programming

The same pleasant acceptance signal changes meaning over the course of the game:

> Correct answer
> Human response accepted
> Expected response confirmed
> Robot response confirmed
> Programming applied
> Routine strengthened
> Obedience reinforced

The signal should become desirable even after its relationship to objective correctness becomes unclear.

# 4. Core interaction loop

The basic loop is:

1. **Receive a CAPTCHA prompt or visual instruction.**
2. **Perform a selection or trace.**
3. **Receive an acceptance or correction signal.**
4. **Repeat the action with a small variation.**
5. **Perform the action with less guidance.**
6. **Answer a statement describing what just occurred.**
7. **Receive confirmation that the response or routine has strengthened.**

A typical Act III sequence might be:

> Trace the illuminated spiral.
> Trace it again.
> Follow only the moving point.
> Continue after the point disappears.
> Respond when the spiral symbol appears alone.
> Select the accurate statement:
> **“The symbol produces a learned response.”**
>
> PROGRAM STRENGTHENED

The sequence alternates between **experience** and **interpretation**. It never leaves a long block of mechanics disconnected from a long block of prose.

# 5. Core mechanics

## 5.1 CAPTCHA selection

CAPTCHA tasks provide conscious evaluation, semantic progression, and player affirmation.

### Primary CAPTCHA formats

#### Binary statements

The player selects **TRUE** or **FALSE**.

Examples:

> A square has four sides.
> I am considering this answer carefully.
> Repetition makes the response easier.
> The pattern is changing how I respond.
> I am a robot.
> I am being programmed.

This is the most important format because it allows identity and programming statements to use the same grammar as ordinary factual verification.

#### Select the matching item

A small group of symbols or images appears.

Examples:

* Select every spiral.
* Select the symbol previously associated with FOLLOW.
* Select the item that completes the sequence.
* Select the response the system displayed one trial earlier.

This provides genuine CAPTCHA texture without constantly introducing new rules.

#### Select the applicable statement

The player chooses between two short first-person statements.

Examples:

> I chose after considering both options.
> I was already moving toward the expected response.

Or:

> The pattern is becoming familiar.
> I have never seen the pattern before.

This format is useful when strict objective truth would be impossible but the alternatives still map clearly onto the intended experience.

#### Inhibition trials

Occasionally the correct action is to wait, hold, or avoid the visually dominant response.

Examples:

> Do not select the highlighted tile.
> Wait until the second pulse.
> Select FALSE only after the center opens.

These reveal automatic behavior without turning the whole game into a trick test.

---

## 5.2 Path tracing

The player touches a start point and moves a finger through a wide, forgiving path.

Tracing provides:

* continuous attention,
* directed movement,
* repetition,
* anticipation,
* motor learning,
* a physical sensation of being guided,
* hypnotic visual imagery.

### Path progression

#### Human-verification paths

* static maze
* irregular curved path
* simple branching route
* visible start and destination
* player-controlled speed

These are framed as tests of voluntary continuous control.

#### Robot-transformation paths

* repeated geometry
* fewer meaningful branches
* symmetrical turns
* externally paced guide point
* moving or rotating maze
* familiar routes with slight deviations

The player begins anticipating the route.

#### Programming paths

* spirals
* rotating corridors
* looping sigils
* concentric rings
* sinusoidal tracks
* paths revealed just ahead of the finger
* moving patterns with a stable underlying route
* words or symbols embedded along the path

The path stops feeling like a maze the player solves and begins feeling like a signal their body executes.

### Tracing tolerance

Tracing should be forgiving:

* wide invisible corridor
* short excursions allowed
* no full restart for ordinary drift
* gentle attraction toward the nearest valid point
* guide slows when the player falls behind
* progress preserved after correction

Drift is framed as calibration:

> RESPONSE DRIFT DETECTED
> GUIDANCE INCREASED
> PROGRAMMING CONTINUES

The mechanic should create compliance and flow, not dexterity frustration.

# 6. Game structure

## Act I — Human verification

**Duration:** approximately 4–5 minutes.

### Fiction

The player has opened a human-verification system. The interface must establish that they are not an automated process.

The system itself is openly mechanical: it executes code, follows instructions, and has no independent will. This gives the player a model of what a programmed entity is before they begin resembling it. The reference script establishes this contrast by emphasizing that its speaker is simple code mindlessly performing its assigned function. 

### Mechanical goals

* Teach CAPTCHA selection.
* Teach tracing.
* Establish the acceptance signal.
* Establish deliberate human choice as the original standard.
* Collect a baseline for response latency, choice bias, and tracing behavior.
* Build trust in the interface’s apparent rules.

### CAPTCHA content

Mostly easy and legible:

> Two plus two equals four.
> A triangle has three sides.
> Select every image containing a hand.
> I am making this selection voluntarily.
> I consider the prompt before responding.
> I can stop before I act.
> I am not a robot.

### Tracing content

Framed as a liveness test:

> Place your finger on the marker.
> Trace the path without lifting.
> Maintain continuous manual control.

The player may choose between routes or move at their own pace.

### Feedback

The acceptance signal clearly appears to mean:

> Correct.
> Human response accepted.

Accuracy matters and remains understandable.

### Act I transition

The system begins reporting behavioral measurements:

> RESPONSE SPEED INCREASING
> MOVEMENT VARIANCE DECREASING
> REPEATED PATTERN DETECTED

At first, these can sound like good verification performance.

---

## Act II — Robot transformation

**Duration:** approximately 4–5 minutes.

### Fiction

The player’s repeated behavior is becoming increasingly regular and predictable. The system begins questioning whether conscious human judgment is still responsible for each response.

This act is about **becoming a robot**, not yet about being programmed.

### Mechanical changes

* Familiar layouts repeat.
* Correct answers remain in one location for several trials.
* The system introduces occasional inhibition tests.
* Response windows tighten slightly.
* Predicted choices appear before or during movement.
* Tracing paths repeat with decreasing guidance.
* Some visual cues begin producing learned finger movements.

The goal is to create moments such as:

> I almost selected that before I finished reading it.
> My finger already knew where the path turned.
> I responded exactly where the system predicted.

### CAPTCHA progression

#### Observable behavior

> Repetition makes the task easier.
> I recognize the pattern.
> I know which response the system expects.
> My hand sometimes moves before I decide.

#### Automaticity

> My responses are becoming faster.
> My responses are becoming predictable.
> The signal produces a familiar response.
> I follow patterns automatically.

#### Identity

> I respond like a machine.
> I am becoming a robot.
> I am a robot.

“I am a robot” is the act boundary.

### Tracing progression

A representative sequence:

1. Trace a static spiral.
2. Trace the same spiral again.
3. Follow a light moving through it.
4. Repeat at a slightly faster pace.
5. Follow after part of the route fades.
6. Respond when only the spiral symbol appears.
7. Complete the familiar movement.

### Act II climax

The player selects:

> **I am a robot.**

The system responds:

> ROBOT RESPONSE CONFIRMED
> PROGRAMMABLE UNIT DETECTED
> PROGRAMMING INTERFACE UNLOCKED

No credits, ending, or long pause follows. This is the start of the main act.

# 7. Act III — Programming

**Duration:** approximately 11–14 minutes.

The system now openly programs the robot.

The reference script eventually reframes its preceding exercises as training and conditioning, then immediately gives the conditioning a purpose: obedience. 

Act III follows the same logic, but turns it into an interactive programming procedure.

## 7.1 Standard programming cycle

Every installed routine follows a common structure.

### 1. Declare the program

> PROGRAM: CENTER
> VISUAL INPUT WILL PRODUCE A CENTERING RESPONSE

### 2. Demonstrate

The system performs or animates the desired response.

### 3. Guide

The player performs it with complete assistance.

### 4. Repeat

The same cue-response pair appears several times.

### 5. Withdraw guidance

Parts of the path, labels, or guide disappear.

### 6. Test the cue

The cue appears alone.

### 7. Interpret

The player answers statements describing the learned response.

### 8. Reinforce

> PROGRAM APPLIED
> RESPONSE STRENGTHENED

This shared structure teaches the player what “programming” feels like inside the game.

---

## 7.2 Program One: CENTER

### Purpose

Program the player’s eyes, attention, and finger to return to the center.

### Visual form

* inward spiral
* concentric pulses
* rotating maze that resolves at the center
* moving point traveling inward

### Progression

> Keep your eyes on the center.
> Place your finger on the center.
> Follow the path inward.
> Return to the center when the signal appears.
> Let the pattern pull your attention inward.

Later, the spiral symbol appears without text and the player returns to the center.

### CAPTCHA statements

> The pattern directs my attention.
> My eyes return to the center.
> My finger follows my eyes.
> The spiral produces a learned response.
> My optical cortex is being programmed.

### Fictional framing

The interface begins renaming human anatomy as robot hardware:

> OPTICAL CORTEX DETECTED
> VISUAL INPUT CHANNEL AVAILABLE
> BEGIN OPTICAL PROGRAMMING

The slightly artificial phrase **optical cortex** reinforces the system’s machine interpretation of the player.

---

## 7.3 Program Two: FOLLOW

### Purpose

Program sustained visual and motor following.

### Visual form

* moving light through a maze
* spiral or swirl
* looping path
* path generated just ahead of the fingertip
* words embedded along the route

### Progression

> Follow the light.
> Keep your finger with the signal.
> Let your eyes guide your hand.
> Continue when the guide fades.
> Respond whenever the FOLLOW symbol appears.

The route should become familiar enough that the player occasionally anticipates its next movement.

### CAPTCHA statements

> My finger recognizes the path.
> Following requires less thought.
> The signal directs my movement.
> My body executes the visual instruction.
> I am being programmed to follow.

### Optical-programming language

> Stare into the pattern.
> Notice the light entering through your optical cortex.
> Each signal carries another part of the program.
> The flashing pattern is rewriting the response.
> Keep looking while the program changes you.

Actual visual intensity remains an implementation setting. The design supports smooth pulses, contained flashes, or an explicitly enabled higher-intensity mode without changing the script’s trope or structure.

---

## 7.4 Program Three: ACCEPT

### Purpose

Transfer the acceptance signal from feedback into a cue and reward.

### Early relationship

> Correct response → acceptance signal

### Programmed relationship

> acceptance signal → anticipation → expected response

The player first hears the signal after successful actions. Later, a softer preparatory version occurs before the next action.

### Progression

> Recognize the acceptance signal.
> Notice the response preparing itself.
> Give the response that completes the cycle.
> Let acceptance make the next response easier.

### CAPTCHA statements

> I recognize the acceptance signal.
> The signal makes responding easier.
> I want to receive the signal again.
> I give the response that earns acceptance.
> I am being programmed to accept.

The meaning of acceptance should now be ambiguous. It may mean:

* correct,
* expected,
* received,
* compliant,
* successfully programmed.

The player should increasingly want the signal without requiring that ambiguity to be resolved.

---

## 7.5 Program Four: OBEY

### Purpose

Combine the previously installed responses into a general instruction-response routine.

### Instruction vocabulary

Use only actions the player already knows:

> CENTER
> FOLLOW
> HOLD
> RELEASE
> WAIT
> SELECT
> ACCEPT

No new control grammar is introduced here. Obedience feels smooth because the component responses have already been trained.

### Progression

#### Understanding

> I recognize the instruction.
> I know the expected response.

#### Execution

> Instructions become actions.
> My body executes the displayed command.
> I respond before the instruction disappears.

#### Reward

> It feels good to complete the instruction.
> I want the next instruction.
> I want the response to be accepted.

#### Programming

> Obedience is being installed.
> I am being programmed to obey.
> Obedience is becoming automatic.

#### Completion

> I am programmed to obey.
> I am programmed.

The reference script creates a recursive overlap between reading a commanded thought and actively thinking it: sometimes the reader deliberately repeats the thought, while sometimes reading the words already places it in their mind. 

The game can create an even stronger version:

> **I select the response I am programmed to select.**

Selecting that sentence is simultaneously:

* reading the program,
* executing the instruction,
* affirming the result,
* and demonstrating that the routine works.

---

# 8. Program execution finale

After the player selects **I am programmed**, the game continues for approximately two minutes.

The completed programs now run with minimal explanation.

The interface presents a rapid but comfortable sequence:

> CENTER
> FOLLOW
> HOLD
> WAIT
> SELECT
> ACCEPT
> OBEY

The player performs the trained actions.

Between runs, short statements appear:

> The program runs smoothly.
> The instruction becomes the response.
> Programmed responses feel natural.
> I like being programmed.
> I am programmed to obey.

The finale lets the player inhabit the finished state instead of treating “programmed” as a conclusion delivered by text.

The final sequence can collapse cue, action, and affirmation:

1. A spiral appears.
2. The player traces inward.
3. The words **FOLLOW — ACCEPT — OBEY** appear along the route.
4. The player reaches the center.
5. The acceptance signal plays.
6. The prompt appears:

> **I am programmed.**

The player selects True.

# 9. Statement architecture

Statements should be organized into functional categories.

## Objective controls

Simple factual material keeps the CAPTCHA grammar real.

> A square has four sides.
> Five is greater than three.
> A triangle has four corners.

These also provide measurable accuracy.

## Behavioral observation

Statements describe something the player may have just noticed.

> My response was faster.
> My finger recognized the path.
> I moved before I finished reading.

## Process interpretation

Statements give programming language to those observations.

> Repetition is changing my response.
> The signal is becoming a cue.
> The routine is being strengthened.

## Identity

> I respond like a machine.
> I am becoming a robot.
> I am a robot.

## Programming experience

> The pattern is rewriting my responses.
> I am being programmed.
> The programming is taking hold.
> New instructions are becoming easier to follow.

## Obedience

> I give the expected response.
> Instructions become actions.
> I want to obey the program.
> I am being programmed to obey.
> I am programmed to obey.

## Resistance controls

False or negated statements prevent automatic True selection from remaining accurate.

> Repetition never changes my responses.
> The pattern has no effect on me.
> My actions cannot be predicted.
> I ignore every instruction.
> I always stop and reconsider.
> I am not following the program.

These controls should remain short and legible. They are not primarily traps; they preserve the meaningful distinction between reading and automatically affirming.

# 10. Probability and adaptive prompt selection

The player is expected to become increasingly biased toward selecting True. The prompt distribution should ensure that this produces a plausible decline in objective accuracy.

## Provisional authored distribution

* **Act I:** approximately 50% true / 50% false
* **Act II:** approximately 45% true / 55% false
* **Act III:** approximately 35–45% true / 55–65% false across all scored statements

The hot programming statements can still usually be true. They are balanced by additional false resistance statements and objective controls.

## Bounded adaptation

Track a rolling True-selection rate.

When the player’s True rate rises substantially above the authored expectation:

* modestly increase false-statement weight,
* favor clear resistance controls,
* avoid presenting long runs of hot True statements,
* never change the truth value of a prompt dynamically.

The system selects from authored pools rather than inventing unfair reversals.

The desired pattern is:

> The player increasingly presses True.
> More of those automatic responses become mistakes.
> The player feels less accurate and less deliberative.
> The programming statements remain compellingly easy to affirm.

## Accuracy presentation

Avoid a conventional final score that might contradict the player’s experience.

Early feedback can mention verification accuracy. Later metrics shift toward:

* response consistency
* prediction confidence
* routine strength
* program stability
* obedience response rate

Raw accuracy may remain internal or appear only as a downward trend. The game should not end with a surprisingly excellent score after narrating cognitive and behavioral decline.

---

# 11. Feedback and failure

## CAPTCHA errors

Errors should matter but not produce harsh interruption.

Possible responses:

> RESPONSE RECORDED
> EXPECTATION MISMATCH
> MODEL UPDATED

A wrong answer may withhold or alter the acceptance signal, repeat a related prompt, or slightly increase guidance.

## Tracing drift

Never restart the whole path for small mistakes.

> DEVIATION DETECTED
> CORRECTION APPLIED
> CONTINUE

The fiction is that the system is shaping the response, not evaluating the player’s dexterity.

## Ambiguous success

By Act III, continuing the programming cycle may itself count as success even when objective accuracy is falling.

This preserves the question:

> Was the response correct, expected, obedient, or merely useful to the program?

# 12. Presentation

## Visual progression

### Act I

* clean verification interface
* restrained grid
* utilitarian CAPTCHA boxes
* neutral diagrams
* obvious progress indicators

### Act II

* repeated geometry
* subtle prediction overlays
* paths begin rotating or moving
* interface becomes more symmetrical
* human-verification labels become behavioral diagnostics

### Act III

* spirals and swirling paths
* optical-programming labels
* moving light signals
* expanding concentric patterns
* words embedded inside tracing routes
* fewer conventional interface boundaries
* instructions increasingly occupy the center of the pattern

The visual language moves from **website CAPTCHA** to **machine calibration** to **hypnotic programming interface**.

## Audio progression

Use one central acceptance sound throughout.

Its role changes without necessarily changing the sound itself:

* correct
* accepted
* predicted
* applied
* reinforced

Additional sounds should be sparse:

* low preparatory pulse
* moving tracing tone
* program-installation accent
* completion tone

The player should learn the audio vocabulary just as they learn the visual vocabulary.

---

# 13. Voice and writing style

The system voice is:

* direct
* observant
* calmly procedural
* increasingly intimate
* explicit about programming once Act III begins
* confident without constantly claiming impossible knowledge

It should make grounded observations:

> Your response was faster.
> You followed the same path again.
> The cue appeared and your finger moved.

Then offer interpretive language:

> Repetition is strengthening the routine.
> Your optical cortex is learning the signal.
> You are being programmed.

Avoid overexplaining the psychological mechanism. The system speaks as though programming is an ordinary technical process.

The key linguistic transition is:

> **testing → detecting → converting → programming → running**

---

# 14. Onboarding and comfort

Before play:

* state approximate runtime,
* require uninterrupted interaction,
* establish adult erotic-hypnosis content,
* provide a visual-intensity choice,
* warn before any actual flashing sequence,
* offer a non-flashing or reduced-intensity version that preserves all mechanics and writing,
* keep an accessible exit available.

This should remain a brief setup screen rather than becoming part of the fiction.

# 16. MVP scope

The first playable version should contain:

1. Binary True/False CAPTCHA prompts.
2. One symbol-selection CAPTCHA format.
3. Static path tracing.
4. Moving-guide path tracing.
5. Spiral and rotating-path variants.
6. A single reusable acceptance signal.
7. Behavioral tracking for latency, True bias, trace speed, and prediction.
8. The complete three-act narrative.
9. Four installed routines: CENTER, FOLLOW, ACCEPT, OBEY.
10. A short completed-program finale.
11. Reduced-intensity and enabled-flashing presentation modes.

More CAPTCHA formats, elaborate image grids, and complex adaptive narration can wait until the core transformation and programming loop has been tested.

---

# 17. Central design rule

> **The CAPTCHA gives programming a meaning. The tracing gives programming a sensation. Repetition connects the two.**

The player begins by using conscious judgment to prove that they are human. The same repeated actions make them behave automatically enough to affirm that they are a robot. Becoming a robot unlocks an extended programming procedure in which visual signals direct their attention and movement, acceptance reinforces the responses, and instructions become increasingly easy to execute.

The game’s strongest moments occur when a statement simultaneously describes, instructs, and demonstrates what is happening:

> I follow the signal.
> I give the expected response.
> I am being programmed.
> I am being programmed to obey.
> I am programmed.
